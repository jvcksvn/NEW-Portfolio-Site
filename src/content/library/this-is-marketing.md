**This is Marketing**, Seth Godin, 2018.

A manifesto for those who already have a hundred people who would miss them, a weak guide for anyone hunting for the first ten. Serving a smallest viable market is correct advice. How zero members becomes one stays unanswered.

Godin's claim is that most marketing fails because it optimizes for mass and average, when the durable path runs through a small group whose worldview a product already matches. Reaching that group takes permission, attention earned rather than bought, a story that fits an existing belief, and consistent showing up rather than a single campaign. Status and belonging, not features, are what people are actually buying, a claim the book treats as settled rather than argued.

What I kept: the question who is it for, asked before any other marketing decision, since most weak campaigns are actually unanswered targeting questions in disguise. And the status distinction, that people buy affiliation, being the kind of person who, as often as they buy a feature, which reframes a lot of stalled positioning problems as identity problems instead of writing problems.

I rewrote the About page for my freelance practice around one sentence naming exactly who it is for and who it is not, and moved every feature claim below the fold. Ten weeks in, the number of inquiries has not moved, four a month, same as before, but the fit has. Three of the last four inquiries named the exact niche in their first message, against zero of the ten before the rewrite. The smallest viable market showed up. It is just not bigger yet, and the book never promised speed, only shape.
