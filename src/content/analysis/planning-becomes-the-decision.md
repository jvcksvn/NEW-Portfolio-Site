# Planning Used to Be Downstream of the Decision. Now It Is the Decision.

For most of its history, production planning was treated as clerical execution. The decisions that mattered, what to sell, where to source, how to price, were made elsewhere, and planning translated them into releases and schedules after the fact. A back office function, measured on schedule attainment, staffed accordingly.

Real time data is collapsing the distance between the decision and the plan. Gartner's 2026 framing of the supply chain control tower, published in late June, puts the planning layer where cost, risk, and customer commitment reconcile continuously rather than in a quarterly review. On the programs I planned, the moments that actually moved money were exactly these reconciliations: a shortage, a cost recovery, and a customer date colliding in the same afternoon, with someone forced to choose which one gave.

## The reconciliation point

When that reconciliation happens in real time, it stops being downstream of anything. Call it the reconciliation point. Planning becomes the place the tradeoffs are made, because it is the only layer that sees inventory, cost, supply, and commitment at the same time. The frame has moved. Planning is no longer a scheduling function that receives decisions. It is the control point where they get made. The reason is structural rather than a matter of anyone's authority: sourcing sees supply, finance sees cost, sales sees the commitment, and none of them sees all three at the instant they collide. Planning does, and once the collision resolves in real time rather than in a monthly meeting, the resolution belongs to whoever is holding that view. This holds only where the data is genuinely integrated. Where it is not, planning is still clerical and the control tower is a slide in a deck.

## What follows

If your planners are measured only on schedule attainment, you are staffing a control tower and scoring it like a data entry desk, and the people best placed to make the tradeoff have no mandate to make it.

The plan used to be where decisions arrived. It is becoming where they are made. The org chart has not caught up yet, and the gap between the two is where the value is currently being left on the floor.
